# Mythological Gods, Demons, and Hybrid Creatures

Use this recipe for any deity, demigod, spirit, demon, or hybrid creature from a specific mythological tradition. The goal is **period-faithful iconography**, not generic fantasy art.

## Prompt scaffold

1. **Subject line** — "[Deity / creature name] of [tradition], depicted in the visual conventions of [period/medium]."
2. **Tradition and period** — be exact. "Inanna/Ishtar in Neo-Assyrian relief convention, c. 700 BCE." "Anubis in Late Period Egyptian temple-relief convention, c. 600 BCE." "Hanuman in Chola bronze convention, c. 1000 CE." This single choice anchors the entire visual.
3. **Canonical attributes** — list the iconographic markers the deity *must* have for the period, drawn from actual surviving depictions:
   - For Inanna (Mesopotamian): horned crown of divinity, rod-and-ring symbol, eight-pointed star, lion as mount/companion, fringed flounced robe.
   - For a lamassu: human head, bearded, horned tiara, body of a winged bull or lion, five legs (so it appears as four from any single viewpoint).
   - For Pazuzu: canine face, scaled body, raptor talons, scorpion tail, four wings, serpent phallus.
   - When unsure, default to "as depicted on contemporary cylinder seals and reliefs of the period."
4. **Form and pose** — describe the pose in period terms: "shown in strict profile striding to the viewer's right, in the Neo-Assyrian relief convention," or "in tribhanga (thrice-bent) pose, frontal, as in Chola bronze."
5. **Medium being emulated** — "as a polychrome glazed-brick relief panel"; "as a carved limestone temple relief with traces of original paint"; "as a fully-rounded cast bronze cult image with green patina"; "as a black-figure vase painting"; "as a Coptic icon panel."
6. **Material and surface** — match step 5 with actual material specificity (glazed brick colors, limestone weathering, bronze patina, etc.).
7. **Composition** — single deity isolated for icon clarity, or deity in narrative scene with attendants/worshippers/enemies.
8. **Framing** — "centered on a neutral seamless background, museum studio light" for icon studies; "in situ on its temple wall, raking lateral light" for context views.
9. **Style register** — "Faithful reconstruction in the visual conventions of [the named medium], museum-quality reference. Photographic if the medium is sculptural; flat and graphic if the medium is painted or incised."
10. **Negative cues** — "no Western-fantasy embellishment, no D&D / video-game / Marvel styling, no exposed cleavage or pin-up posing unless period-attested, no neon glows, no anachronistic weaponry, no random horns/wings beyond what the canonical iconography prescribes, no modern tattoos."

## Aspect ratio

- Single standing deity / icon study → `3:4`
- Narrative scene with multiple figures → `16:9`
- Relief panel emulation → `4:3` or `16:9` per actual surviving examples
- Centered seal or square panel → `1:1`

## Worked example prompt

```
Inanna/Ishtar of Mesopotamia, depicted in Neo-Assyrian polychrome glazed-brick relief convention, c. 700 BCE.

Canonical attributes: horned crown of divinity (multiple tiered horns), rod-and-ring symbol held in the right hand, eight-pointed star above the right shoulder, lion couchant beneath her feet as her companion mount, long fringed flounced robe with multiple tiered tufts, weapons rising from her shoulders (a quiver and the hilts of curved blades) marking her as a warrior goddess.

Form and pose: shown in strict left-facing profile, striding forward, in the Neo-Assyrian relief convention (head and legs in profile, torso slightly turned toward the viewer, eye almond-shaped and frontal).

Medium being emulated: polychrome glazed-brick relief panel of the kind preserved from the Ishtar Gate and Processional Way of Babylon — though here on a notional Assyrian palace wall.

Material and surface: deep cobalt-blue background glaze, figure in turquoise, ochre-yellow, white, and manganese black, slight surface crazing in the glaze, faint mortar lines between bricks.

Composition: single deity isolated, centered, full-length, occupying the full height of a tall rectangular panel; small cuneiform-style decorative border top and bottom (purely ornamental, no readable text).

Framing: flat-on orthographic view of the panel, edge-to-edge.

Lighting: even raking sidelight from the upper left, just enough to read the low relief.

Style: faithful reconstruction in the visual conventions of Neo-Assyrian/Babylonian glazed-brick relief, museum-quality reference, flat and graphic.

Negative: no Western-fantasy embellishment, no D&D / video-game / Marvel styling, no pin-up posing, no neon glows, no anachronistic weaponry, no random horns/wings beyond canonical iconography, no modern tattoos, no readable modern lettering.
```

## Common follow-ups to offer

- The same deity in a *different* medium for comparison (e.g. cylinder-seal impression vs. glazed brick).
- A narrative scene (e.g. "Inanna descending to the underworld, scene at the first gate").
- A companion deity from the same pantheon, rendered in the same medium, to begin a matched icon set.
- A line-art "iconographic key" version labeling each attribute (only if explicitly requested — otherwise honor the "no text" rule).

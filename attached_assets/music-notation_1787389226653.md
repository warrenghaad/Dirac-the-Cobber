# Music Notation, Instruments & Performance

Use this recipe for any musical visual — Western staff notation, modal/maqam diagrams, fretboard and keyboard layouts, chord voicings, rhythm grids, instrument studies (historical or modern), performance scenes, and notation-system comparisons across civilizations (cuneiform hymn tablets, neumes, jianpu, Indian sargam, Arabic maqam scrolls).

## Prompt scaffold

1. **Subject line** — "[Notation system / instrument / performance moment], [tradition/period if relevant]."
2. **Notation system specificity** — name it exactly:
   - "Modern Western five-line staff in treble clef"
   - "Lead-sheet style: melody on staff with chord symbols above (e.g. Cmaj7, D-7, G7alt)"
   - "Jazz Real Book hand-lettered convention"
   - "Arabic maqam scroll notation with quarter-tone accidentals (half-flat, half-sharp)"
   - "Byzantine neume notation on a single horizontal line"
   - "Hurrian cult hymn tablet style — cuneiform on fired clay"
   - "Indian sargam syllables (Sa Re Ga Ma Pa Dha Ni) on a single line with octave dots"
   - "Guitar tablature (six lines, fret numbers)"
3. **Musical content** — exact pitches, rhythms, intervals, or scale. Do not let the model invent — specify. ("C Dorian scale ascending one octave," "ii-V-I in F major with rootless A-form voicings," "maqam Rast tetrachord on C: C D E-half-flat F").
4. **Layout** — "single line of notation, edge-to-edge"; "four-bar phrase with measure numbers"; "scale diagram with degree numbers below each note"; "fretboard with frets 1-12 visible and the scale tones marked as filled circles, roots as red circles."
5. **Instrument or performer (if shown)** — instrument type, period-correct construction, player posture, hand position, attire. For historical instruments, name actual surviving examples ("a lyre of Ur, c. 2500 BCE, with bull's-head soundbox in lapis-and-gold inlay").
6. **Material register** — "printed engraver-quality notation, crisp black ink on cream paper, in the visual style of Henle / Bärenreiter editions"; "hand-lettered in the style of a jazz Real Book"; "ink on parchment with period-correct mistakes and corrections"; "incised cuneiform on fired clay tablet, raking light."
7. **Lighting and framing** — for pure notation: "flat-on orthographic, even diffuse lighting, no perspective." For instruments: "three-quarter studio view, soft museum light." For performance: cinematic per the scenes recipe.
8. **Style register** — "Museum-quality notation reference plate" / "Studio product photograph" / "Documentary performance photograph."
9. **Negative cues** — "no garbled or invented notation symbols, no random extra clefs or time signatures, no misspelled note names, no anachronistic mixing of notation systems, no neon, no glowing magic notes, no Disney conducting clichés."

## Aspect ratio

- Single line of staff / scale → `16:9`
- Multi-bar phrase, four to eight bars → `16:9` or `4:3`
- Fretboard / keyboard diagram → `16:9`
- Single chord voicing on a grid → `1:1`
- Instrument portrait → `3:4`
- Performance scene → `16:9`
- Comparative chart of notation systems (3-4 rows) → `4:3` or `1:1`

## Model

- Default: `gpt_image_2` (best for legible notation symbols).
- **Important caveat:** all image models hallucinate the exact notes. For *correct* notation, generate a clean visual scaffold here, then have the user (or a follow-up call) render the actual notes with a music-engraving tool (LilyPond / MuseScore). Always tell the user when notation accuracy matters.

## Common follow-ups to offer

- A comparative four-row chart: Western staff vs. Arabic maqam vs. Indian sargam vs. Hurrian cuneiform, all showing the *same* underlying pitch sequence.
- An instrument portrait of the period-appropriate instrument that plays this scale (oud, lyre of Ur, sitar, guitar).
- A fretboard map of the same scale on guitar.
- A performance scene of a musician playing this scale in a period-correct setting.
- A LilyPond/MuseScore render handoff for publication-quality notation.

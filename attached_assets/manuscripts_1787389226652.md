# Manuscripts, Tablets & Written Artifacts

Use this recipe for clay tablets, papyri, parchment codices, illuminated manuscripts, palm-leaf manuscripts, scrolls, ostraca, inscribed stelae, and any other written artifact studied as a *physical object* (not as a text to be read). The goal is a museum-grade study image of the medium itself.

## Prompt scaffold

1. **Subject line** — "[Manuscript type] from [tradition], [period], [find context if any]."
2. **Tradition, period, region** — exact: "Old Babylonian school tablet, c. 1800 BCE, Nippur"; "Byzantine illuminated Gospel codex, Constantinople, 11th c."; "Mamluk Qur'an folio in muhaqqaq script, Cairo, 14th c."; "Mexica/Aztec screenfold codex on amate paper, early 16th c."; "South Indian palm-leaf manuscript in Grantha script, 17th c."
3. **Script and language** — name the script *system* without inventing readable text: "cuneiform in Sumerian/Akkadian, signs arranged in horizontal cases divided by ruled lines"; "minuscule Greek with abundant ligatures and abbreviations"; "naskh / muhaqqaq / kufic Arabic"; "Demotic Egyptian"; "Nahuatl glyphic logograms with red and black outlines." **Important:** do not request "readable" text — image models cannot render correct historical scripts. Specify "plausible signs, no requirement of textual accuracy."
4. **Layout convention** — column count, ruling, margins, rubrication, marginalia, catchwords, illumination program. ("Two-column page with red-ruled margins, gilded initials, marginal commentary in a smaller hand, foliate border on the outer margin only.")
5. **Material and condition** — be specific:
   - "Fired clay tablet, 8×10 cm, palm-sized, slightly convex, edges chipped, surface scratches"
   - "Vellum (calfskin), prepared cream side facing the viewer, faint hair-side follicle dots, soft cockling at the edges"
   - "Papyrus, horizontal fibers on the recto, faint vertical grain showing through, one horizontal crack repaired with a thin strip"
   - "Palm leaf, narrow rectangular strip with a single binding hole, smoked surface, incised text rubbed with lampblack"
6. **Pigments and inks** (when applicable) — iron-gall ink (warm brown-black), lampblack carbon ink (dense cool black), vermilion rubrication, ultramarine and gold leaf for illumination, indigo, malachite green, cochineal/kermes red.
7. **Framing** — pick one:
   - "Flat-on orthographic top-down, edge-to-edge, museum scan style"
   - "Three-quarter studio view, slight angle, showing the dimensionality of the page/tablet"
   - "Open codex spread, both pages visible, gentle spine valley"
   - "Detail close-up of one decorated initial / one cuneiform case / one illumination"
8. **Lighting** — "even diffuse museum-scan light, no shadows, neutral 5000K" for orthographic plates; "soft raking light from upper left, low-contrast" for three-quarter views that need to show texture.
9. **Style register** — "Museum-quality study image of a written artifact, materially accurate, ultra-sharp" or "Archaeological reconstruction of the manuscript as originally made, before later damage."
10. **Negative cues** — "no readable modern fonts, no Latin alphabet sneaking into non-Latin scripts, no anachronistic page numbers in Arabic numerals, no museum labels in frame, no QR codes, no rulers, no fantasy embellishment, no glowing magical illumination, no D&D 'ancient scroll' aesthetic, no aged-paper-texture stock-photo cliché."

## Aspect ratio

- Single tablet, isolated → `1:1` or `4:3`
- Single codex page → `3:4`
- Open codex spread → `16:9`
- Scroll or screenfold extended horizontally → `16:9`
- Detail close-up of one element → `1:1`

## Model

- Default: `gpt_image_2`.
- For a cutout study image (tablet floating on transparent background, drop-in asset for curriculum slides), use `gpt_image_1_5` with `background: "transparent"`.

## Worked example prompt

```
An Old Babylonian school tablet, c. 1800 BCE, from a scribal school at Nippur.

Script and content: cuneiform signs in Sumerian, arranged in two columns of horizontal cases separated by faint ruled lines. The tablet is a "type II" lentil — round, palm-sized, with a teacher's model line at the top and a student's repeated copy below. Cuneiform signs shown as plausible wedge configurations; no requirement of textual accuracy.

Layout: round lenticular obverse with horizontal ruled cases, one column of signs at the top half (teacher), a near-duplicate column below (student), slightly less precisely formed.

Material and condition: fired clay, warm tan color with darker streaks where the firing was uneven, surface slightly convex, edges worn and lightly chipped, a single hairline crack across the lower third, fingerprints faintly preserved at the rim where the scribe held it.

Framing: three-quarter studio view from slightly above, showing the dimensionality of the disk and the depth of the wedge impressions.

Lighting: soft raking light from the upper left at a low angle so each cuneiform wedge casts a tiny crisp shadow, gentle fill from the right, neutral seamless gray-cream background.

Style: museum-quality study image of a written artifact, materially accurate, ultra-sharp.

Negative: no readable modern fonts, no Latin alphabet, no anachronistic page numbers, no museum labels, no QR codes, no rulers, no fantasy embellishment, no glowing magical script, no aged-paper-texture stock cliché.
```

Aspect ratio: `4:3`. Model: `gpt_image_2`. Filename: `nippur_school_tablet_lentil_studio_v1`.

## Common follow-ups to offer

- A flat-on orthographic museum-scan companion plate of the same tablet.
- A detail macro of a single case of signs.
- A reverse-side view (most school tablets were inscribed on both faces).
- A scene of the student-scribe at work in the edubba, using this tablet (handoff to the `scenes.md` recipe).
- A comparative plate placing this tablet next to a Mamluk Qur'an folio and a Nahuatl screenfold page for cross-civilization writing-medium comparison.

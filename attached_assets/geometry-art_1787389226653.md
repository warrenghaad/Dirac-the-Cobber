# Geometric Art, Patterns & Iconography

Use this recipe for patterns, tilings, motifs, ornament, single-symbol studies, glyphs, and civilization-specific decorative geometry.

## Prompt scaffold

1. **Subject line** — "[Civilization/period]-style geometric [pattern / motif / single symbol] depicting [structure]."
2. **Period and region** — be exact (e.g. "Neo-Sumerian Ur III, c. 2100 BCE"; "Nasrid Granada, 14th c."; "Late Heian Japan, 11th c."; "Cosmati Rome, 12th c."; "Anatolian Seljuk, 13th c.").
3. **Underlying geometry** — name the construction: "based on a 6-fold rosette grid," "interlocking 12-pointed stars on a hexagonal lattice," "guilloche of two interwoven bands," "square-on-square rotation at 22.5°," "Borromean three-ring interlace."
4. **Color palette** — specific pigments/materials of the period: "lapis blue, ochre, hematite red on cream gesso," "indigo and madder on undyed wool," "polychrome glazed faience (turquoise, cobalt, manganese black)."
5. **Material and surface** — "incised on fired clay tile," "inlaid stone (cosmati) on marble," "woven wool kilim," "tooled on hammered bronze plaque," "painted on lime plaster."
6. **Repeat behavior** — "single rosette centered" or "seamless tile, full repeat shown 3×3" or "border strip running horizontally" or "corner-and-field composition."
7. **Framing** — "flat-on, orthographic top-down view, edge-to-edge" for patterns; "isolated on neutral seamless background, raking light" for single symbols.
8. **Style register** — pick one:
   - "Museum reference plate, ultra-sharp, evenly lit, no perspective distortion"
   - "Documentary archaeological photograph of the surviving artifact"
   - "Faithful reconstruction painting in the visual conventions of the period"
9. **Negative cues** — "no modern fonts, no labels, no measurement marks, no fantasy embellishment, no New-Age geometry-book aesthetics, no rainbow gradients, no neon, no 3D bevels."

## Aspect ratio

- Repeating pattern, seamless tile → `1:1`
- Border or frieze strip → `16:9` (or `9:16` rotated)
- Single symbol/glyph study → `1:1`
- Pattern on artifact surface in context → `4:3`

## Worked example prompt

```
Neo-Sumerian Ur III-style geometric rosette pattern (c. 2100 BCE, Mesopotamia).

Underlying geometry: 8-petal rosette inscribed in a square, with the petals constructed from intersecting circles on an octagonal grid; the rosette is centered in a square panel and surrounded by a guilloche border of two interwoven bands.

Color palette: lapis blue, deep ochre, hematite red, and cream, faithful to surviving Mesopotamian wall-painting and inlay pigments.

Material and surface: faithful reconstruction as painted lime plaster on a temple wall, with subtle aging and faint craquelure.

Repeat behavior: single panel, centered rosette with full border visible.

Framing: flat-on orthographic top-down view, edge-to-edge, no perspective distortion.

Style: museum reference plate, ultra-sharp, evenly lit, photographic clarity.

Negative: no modern fonts, no labels, no measurement marks, no fantasy embellishment, no New-Age geometry-book aesthetics, no rainbow gradients, no neon, no 3D bevels.
```

Aspect ratio: `1:1`. Model: `gpt_image_2`. Filename: `sumerian_8petal_rosette_panel_v1`.

## Common follow-ups to offer

- A seamless tileable version of the same motif.
- A line-art "construction diagram" variant showing the underlying grid.
- The same motif rendered in a different civilization's idiom (e.g. Nasrid vs. Sumerian) for cross-cultural comparison.
- A textile/kilim variant of the same geometry.
